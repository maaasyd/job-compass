# Hand-off

After step 4 (or whenever the person wants to stop), give them a short summary in the conversation. Offer it as a document or page if they'd like to keep it. It should fit on one phone screen. In helper mode, the helper will forward it, so write it as plain text that can be pasted into a message as it is, with no bold or other formatting marks, or offer it as a page.

## What goes in

1. **What you liked.** Two or three sentences in their words where possible: the kinds of work, the tasks they kept picking, and the settings that mattered (hours, place, team size).
2. **Search words.** 5 to 8 job titles and terms to type into job sites, taken from the quiz results and the ads they liked. Give a short plain meaning for any title that doesn't explain itself, as on the quiz page. If a title usually pays below the pay they need, say so next to it. If you leave out a kind of work that came out near the top, say why in a few words. Include one or two in the local language if they searched in another country. Add the filters that matter to them (location, hours). If a route needs a course or certificate first, say how long that takes, so they can see whether it fits the time frame they gave.
3. **Must or wish, one line.** The signal words they found hardest, as a reminder. For example: "'preferred', 'ideally' and 'is a plus' mean a wish, not a condition."
4. **One small next step, if they want one.** Seeing the work up close helps, but it should feel light. Name one option, two at most, that suits the person, with a link where there is one:
   - a short video of a normal day in this job, made by someone who does it ("day in the life" plus the job title usually finds one)
   - first-hand accounts: people describing the work in posts, interviews or forums for that field
   - a trial day, a taster day or a day shadowing someone, which some employers, colleges and training providers offer
   - the free public careers service where they live, which helps school leavers and adults changing direction (in Germany, the Berufsberatung at the Arbeitsagentur)

   Talking to someone who does the work is useful too, but don't present it as the expected next step, because many people find it a big hurdle. If they want to, three questions help: "What did you do yesterday, from start to finish?", "What surprised you about this work?" and "How did people you know get their first job in it?"
5. **When they're ready to apply.** Applications, CVs and interviews are outside this skill. Name it plainly ("When you're ready to apply, a CV and cover-letter tool is the next step") and stop there.

## Tone
- No pressure. "When you're ready" rather than "Next week, apply to…".
- No verdicts. "You kept picking organising tasks" rather than "You are an organiser".
- If they found nothing they liked, that's a result too. Summarise what they ruled out and offer to start again with new kinds of work whenever they want.

## Example

> **What you liked.** Of everything you looked at, you kept coming back to teaching and to cooking in daytime settings. The tasks you picked were mostly about showing people how to do things and running a small team. Regular daytime hours mattered more than pay.
>
> **Search words.** Chef Trainer · Catering Lecturer · Apprenticeship Trainer (hospitality) · School Cook · Kitchen Manager (schools) · Catering Supervisor. Filter for full-time, Monday to Friday, within 40 minutes.
>
> **Must or wish.** "Desirable", "ideally" and "would be an advantage" mean a wish.
>
> **One small next step.** If you'd like to see the work up close, watch a short "day in the life" video of a chef trainer, or ask a catering college whether you could spend a day with one of their trainers.
