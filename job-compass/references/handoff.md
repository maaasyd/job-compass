# Hand-off

After stage 3 (or whenever the person wants to stop), give them a short summary in the conversation. Offer it as a document or page if they'd like to keep it. It should fit on one phone screen.

## What goes in

1. **What you liked.** Two or three sentences in their words where possible: the kinds of work, the tasks they kept picking, and the settings that mattered (hours, place, team size).
2. **Search words.** 5 to 8 job titles and terms to type into job sites, taken from the quiz results and the ads they liked. Include one or two in the local language if they searched in another country. Add the filters that matter to them (location, hours).
3. **Must or wish, one line.** The signal words they found hardest, as a reminder. For example: "'preferred', 'ideally' and 'is a plus' mean a wish, not a condition."
4. **Talk to one person.** The strongest next step is a 20-minute conversation with someone who does this work. Suggest how to find one (a former classmate, a friend of a friend, a professional association, a polite message on a professional network) and three questions to ask:
   - "What did you do yesterday, from start to finish?"
   - "What surprised you about this work?"
   - "How did people you know get their first job in it?"
5. **What's next, if they want it.** Applications, CVs and interviews are outside this skill. Name it plainly ("When you're ready to apply, a CV and cover-letter tool is the next step") and stop there.

## Tone
- No pressure. "When you're ready" rather than "Next week, apply to…".
- No verdicts. "You kept picking organising tasks" rather than "You are an organiser".
- If they found nothing they liked, that's a result too. Summarise what they ruled out and offer to start again with new kinds of work whenever they want.

## Example

> **What you liked.** Of everything you looked at, you kept coming back to teaching and to cooking in daytime settings. The tasks you picked were mostly about showing people how to do things and running a small team. Regular daytime hours mattered more than pay.
>
> **Search words.** Chef Trainer · Catering Lecturer · Apprenticeship Trainer (hospitality) · School Cook · Kitchen Manager (schools) · Catering Supervisor. Filter for full-time, Monday to Friday, within 40 minutes.
>
> **Must or wish.** "Desirable", "ideally" and "would be an advantage" mean a wish.
>
> **Talk to one person.** Someone who trains apprentice chefs, or who runs a school kitchen. Ask what they did yesterday, what surprised them, and how they got in.
