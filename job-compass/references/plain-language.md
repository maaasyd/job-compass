# Plain language

Everything the page says in its own voice must be clear to someone outside the field, reading on a phone, maybe tired or anxious. That covers names, descriptions, tasks, explanations and notes. Jargon is allowed in exactly two places: inside quotes from an ad, and as the terms in a word list, where it gets explained.

## Rules
- **Short sentences.** Aim for under 20 words. The checker warns above 30.
- **"You" and verbs.** "You check the numbers" rather than "Responsible for data validation".
- **Everyday words.** "Use" not "utilise", "help" not "facilitate", "the people involved" not "stakeholders". `assets/jargon.txt` lists the usual offenders, and the checker flags them.
- **Explain every abbreviation** the first time, or put it in the word list. The checker flags unexplained capitals.
- **No status talk.** Don't call one kind of work "strategic" or "senior" and another "basic".
- **No hype and no cheerleading.** "Great news!", "You'd be perfect!" and "Exciting opportunity" all go. A calm, specific sentence is kinder.
- **Honest about gaps.** "This is the one line where you'd be at the low end." Then add context only if it's true.
- **Don't make promises.** Never say they'd get the job or that a requirement won't matter. Say "worth asking" instead.
- **Numbers as numbers**, money with its currency, and dates written out.
- **One idea per sentence** in descriptions. Lists of three adjectives say nothing.

## Writing like a person
The page should sound like a friend who knows the field, explaining it over coffee:
- Contractions are fine: "you'd", "it's", "don't".
- Vary sentence length a little. Not every sentence needs a colon or a list of three.
- Use concrete examples: "a spicy lentil soup that sells for under £2" beats "customer requirements".
- Keep the warmth in what you choose to say, not in adjectives.

## Check yourself
After writing a page's data, read every `day`, `short`, `week`, `why` and `know` line aloud. If you'd need to explain a word to a friend, explain it on the page or replace it.
